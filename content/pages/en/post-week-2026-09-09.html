Week of September 9, 2026

Guide polaritons and think about heat

From September 9 to 15, 2026

This week we review how an interface can guide confined waves, how a ferroelectric transistor can take on various computing functions, and what happens to thermal transport when a ferroelectric nitride is modified.

Losses as a tool to guide a wave

In photonics, attempts are often made to reduce losses, because a signal that is absorbed or leaked is no longer available where it is needed. However, when loss depends on direction and the material underneath, it can also modify which paths remain accessible to a wave.

Hongyi Yuan and his collaborators, with participation from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Hunan University in China, explore this possibility with a sheet of α-MoO₃ on β-Ga₂O₃. Phonon-polaritons, hybrid waves of light and vibration of the material, propagate in the sheet, while the substrate introduces leakage channels whose influence changes with frequency.

The authors observe through near-field microscopy that the direction of propagation can rotate as the frequency varies and describe an almost one-dimensional regime of the resulting waves. They also investigate other combinations of materials, including α-MoO₃ on CdWO₄, to show that the effect can appear in different infrared regions.

The work proposes using an interface usually seen as a source of dissipation to design the propagation, although this does not eliminate the fact that the signal loses energy. Furthermore, demonstrations are performed on specific heterostructures and on specific bands, so converting this mechanism into a practical circuit will require evaluating useful distance, coupling, and ease of fabrication along with directional control.

H. Yuan et al., “Loss-engineered reconfigurable polaritons,” Nature Communications, vol. 17, art. 10658, 2026. Full article. E-publishing September 9, 2026.

Filtering before activating a spiking network

A neural network that receives a complete image may spend resources processing regions that are not relevant to the task. In spiking systems, that unnecessary activity also means activating more elements, so deciding what information passes to the network can be as important as the operation of the network itself.

Lingqi Li and his collaborators, with researchers from National University of Singapore and A*STAR, in Singapore, study double-gate transistors with a MoS₂ channel and a hafnium zirconium oxide ferroelectric layer. Depending on the signals applied, the devices can display logical functions and behaviors that the authors use as blocks of neurons or artificial synapses.

The proposed architecture uses logical operations to select part of the input before sending it to a spiking network. In a digit classification simulation, with parameters adjusted to the device measurements, the authors obtain a 95.9% success rate on MNIST after 50 epochs. They also explore more complex tasks, for which increasing network size and simulation time improves accuracy, but increases estimated consumption.

The distinction between the measured device and the system evaluation is essential. The architecture-scale energy calculation uses a model calibrated with operating data and excludes peripheral circuitry, so its savings are not a complete measure of the consumption of a finished processor. Even so, the combination of filtering and neuromorphic functions in the same family of devices offers a concrete way to study what work should be avoided before calculating.

L. Li et al., “A spiking neural network-in-logic architecture based on reconfigurable molybdenum disulfide dual-gate transistors with ferroelectric gating,” Nature Electronics, vol. 9, pp. 1110–1121, 2026. Full article. E-publishing September 14, 2026.

A ferroelectric that also has to evacuate heat

When new material is introduced into a memory, it is easy to first notice how its polarization changes and the voltage required to write it. However, the heat generated by the device also has to leave, and modifications that facilitate switching can affect that other property.

Sara Makarem and her collaborators, from the University of Virginia and Pennsylvania State University in the United States, study ferroelectric films based on aluminum nitride doped with boron and, in some cases, scandium. They compare compositions and thicknesses using thermal transport measurements and infrared ellipsometry, with which they analyze the response of optical vibrations of the crystalline lattice.

The results show that the disorder introduced by the composition modifies the phonon scattering and can reduce the thermal conductivity in the direction perpendicular to the film. In the samples examined, the authors also found a relationship between this conductivity and the lifetime of an optical mode measured by ellipsometry. This relationship offers a characterization tool for this system, without establishing a universal rule for all ferroelectrics.

The trade-off is relevant to memory and logic design, because a modification that reduces the field needed to reverse polarization can also make heat removal more difficult. Before choosing a composition for a specific device, it will be necessary to consider both properties together with the geometry and the rest of the circuit layers.

S. Makarem et al., “Thermal conductivity and optical phonon scattering of CMOS compatible aluminum nitride based ferroelectric thin films,” Nature Communications, 2026. Full article. E-publishing September 11, 2026.
