Week of March 11, 2026

Mixing light and magnetism, storing charge and watching an LED age

A flat band can strengthen the interaction between light and magnetism. In another chip, putting memory near the optics changes the path the data take, while an electron microscope shows where a working LED starts to degrade.

Photonics

When a flat band helps mix light and magnetism

[1] Q. Hong, J. Qian, F. Chen, Y. Yang, and Y.-P. Wang, “Strong magnon–photon coupling enhanced by photonic lattice flat-bands,” Nature Communications, vol. 17, no. 1, Art. no. 2438, Mar. 13, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-026-69326-y.

Getting an electromagnetic wave to exchange energy with a magnetic material is relatively simple. Much more delicate is to make this exchange intense enough so that both stop behaving as independent systems and form hybrid states. The difficulty increases when the photonic structure grows, since the field tends to spread out and the local interaction can weaken.

A team from Zhejiang University and East China Normal University, both in China, has studied a way to address this problem using a network of resonators with a flat band. Many modes with similar frequencies appear in it that, when combined, can concentrate their interaction with a small sphere of yttrium iron garnet. The researchers tune the magnetic response of the sphere with an external field and observe the coupling with the electromagnetic modes of the lattice.

With twelve cells, the hybrid modes split by 180 MHz, compared with 92 MHz in the reference structure. Adding cells might be expected to spread the field over a larger region and weaken the local coupling. In the microwave measurements, the flat band lets the coupling persist as the lattice grows.

Electronics

Electronic memory beside photonic computing

[2] S. Lam et al., “Neuromorphic photonic computing with an electro-optic analog memory,” Nature Communications, vol. 17, no. 1, Art. no. 2472, Mar. 16, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-026-69084-x.

Participating institutions
University of British Columbia (Canada)

In a photonic processor, moving and converting the values that control light also uses energy. The team puts a capacitor-based analogue memory on the same chip so each value can be kept next to the unit using it.

Capacitors lose charge over time, so the authors examine how much the values can drift before digit classification suffers. Their energy analysis estimates how much shortening that path could save compared with digital memory and converters.

Materials

Watching a working LED degrade under a microscope

[3] X. Li et al., “Multimodal electron microscopy of halide perovskite interfacial dynamics,” Nature, vol. 651, no. 8106, pp. 614–620, Mar. 11, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41586-026-10238-8.

Participating institutions
University of Cambridge (United Kingdom)

The team biases a perovskite diode inside an electron microscope and combines measurements sensitive to composition, structure and strain. It can then see where the first changes appear as the device operates.

The changes cluster near the charge transport layers. Lead-rich regions form and grains fragment, while part of the aluminium contact turns into an insulating compound. The interior of the emitting layer holds up better, directing attention to the interfaces.
