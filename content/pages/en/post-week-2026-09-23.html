Week of September 23, 2026

Cavities on a fiber, graphene electrodes and ion channels

Week of September 23 to 29, 2026.

Photonics

Building a network of resonators on the surface of a fiber

N. Fried, D. L. P. Vitullo and A. Dutt, “Multiband topological heterojunctions on the surface nanoscale axial photonics platform”, Nature Communications, vol. 17, art. 10071, 2026. Epub September 29. Open Access Article.

The work includes researchers from the University of Maryland, in the United States.

A chain of coupled resonators makes it possible to study how light moves in periodic structures and what happens when regions with different properties come together. Fried and his collaborators build these networks using the surface of an optical fiber, on a platform where minute variations in its effective radius create cavities along the axis.

The team couples neighboring resonators to form photonic networks inspired by the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger model and studies junctions between regions with different topological properties. The platform allows the cavities to be locally adjusted after characterizing them, an advantage when small manufacturing differences shift their frequencies and make the desired coupling difficult. Furthermore, each axial mode offers another band with which to explore the response of the structure.

The demonstration serves to investigate topological photonics phenomena in a low-loss system, although a network of resonators on fiber should not be confused with a planar integrated circuit. Their interest is in offering another experimental environment where they can control the coupling and precisely compare the observed modes with the models.

Electronics

A memristor where two graphene edges intersect

Y. Liu et al., “Stress-engineered 0.34 nm graphene memristors for low-power probabilistic computing”, Nature Communications, 2026. E-publishing September 23. Open Access Article.

The team brings together researchers from Tsinghua University and Fudan University, in China.

Reducing the size of a memory cell requires more than just reducing a conductive area, because you also have to make electrodes that can be reproduced and connected without destroying the material. Liu and his collaborators work with crossed graphene edges and describe a transfer procedure that controls mechanical stresses to place the sheet on vertical walls.

The two-edge geometry of a layer defines a nominal crossing region of 0.34 by 0.34 nanometers. In the devices studied, the authors observed volatile switching and a ratio between states greater than one thousand, and used their variability in a demonstration of probabilistic bits with a reported power of 370 nanowatts under the conditions tested.

That dimension corresponds to the nominal geometric crossover region, not the size of the entire device or an entire memory system. The volatile response can be useful when looking for fluctuations for probabilistic calculation, although evaluating density, peripheral consumption and uniformity in practical matrices remains a pending step. The journal shows an early version that may change during the final edition.

Materials

Allow ions to pass without water doing the same

Y. Wang et al., “Rapid-responsive and water-stable organic electrochemical transistors enabled by supramolecular artificial ion channels”, Nature Materials, 2026. E-publishing September 25. Open Access Article.

Among the participating institutions is the University of Pennsylvania, in the United States.

Organic electrochemical transistors convert ionic signals into changes in electrical current, an attractive property for working in contact with aqueous media. However, water that uncontrollably penetrates the active materials can modify their structure and limit both the speed and stability of the device. Wang and his collaborators propose a layer that facilitates the passage of ions while reducing water permeation.

The material combines supramolecular stacks of crown ethers with a fluorinated polymer matrix. In their measurements, the authors obtained a response time constant of 1.3 microseconds for a specific configuration and studied the stability of the device in aqueous solutions. The work also includes an experimental demonstration of detection in an animal model, with which it explores a possible bioelectronic application.

The separation between ionic transport and water entry offers a useful guideline for designing interfaces between electronics and biological media, although performance depends on the semiconductor, electrolyte and architecture used.
