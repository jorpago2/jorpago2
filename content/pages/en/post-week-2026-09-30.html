Week of September 30, 2026

Ultra-thin layers, three-dimensional memory and stable ceramics

From September 30 to October 6, 2026

Two crystals to select circular polarization

Circular polarization can be distinguished by combining an element that delays two components of light differently with another that selects a direction. This usually requires several components, so reducing its thickness is attractive when trying to integrate this function with a source or detector.

Researchers at Peking University in China have combined two lamellar crystals with different optical responses. An NbOCl₂ layer introduces a delay between polarization components and another MoOCl₂ layer preferentially absorbs one of them. By stacking them in a proper orientation, the set distinguishes between the two circular polarizations of light.

The authors characterize the transmission and present an integration test with an LED. The selection depends on the wavelength and the geometry of the layers, so the measured performances must be interpreted under these conditions. The proposal is interesting because it takes advantage of the natural anisotropy of the crystals to bring together two optical functions in an assembly with a thickness of less than one micron, without requiring the etching of a metasurface.

S. Huang et al., «Ultrathin high-performance circular polarizers based on MoOCl₂/NbOCl₂ van der Waals heterostructures», Nature Communications, published electronically on October 6, 2026. Open Access. DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-78564-z. The reading is based on the version available after the first electronic publication.

A stacked memristor and transistor for signal processing

Some tasks depend on both the order and timing of signals, so a device whose response evolves over time can be useful as part of a computing system. Reservoir computing explores precisely how to transform those inputs into dynamic responses that can then be interpreted with a readout stage.

A team from Seoul National University and Yonsei University, South Korea, has fabricated a structure that stacks a memristor and thin-film transistors. The vertical arrangement reduces the footprint of the unit, while the gate voltage allows part of the circuit's temporal response to be adjusted.

The researchers build an 8-by-16-unit array and explore modes of operation applied to motion and ultrasound signals. In the measurement setup, an external capacitor is also used to establish the temporal dynamics, a necessary detail to assess what is integrated and what belongs to the test circuit. The work shows a way to combine memory and control in a stacked architecture, although it will still be necessary to study how peripheral resources evolve when expanding the system.

S. K. Shim et al., «Monolithic three-dimensionally integrated memristor-thin-film transistor for electrically programmable multimode reservoir computing», Nature Communications, published electronically October 3, 2026. Open Access. DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-78364-5. The reading is based on the version available after the first electronic publication.

A dielectric that tries not to change with temperature

When reducing the size of a capacitor, it is important that its material stores charge efficiently, loses little energy and maintains stable behavior when the temperature changes. It is difficult to optimize all three properties at once, and the number of possible compositions makes testing them all in the laboratory impractical.

Junlei Qi and his collaborators have combined machine learning, crystal family selection and experimental fabrication to search for oxides with that balance of properties. Among the participating institutions is Tsinghua University, in China. After studying different compositions, the team prepares a ceramic with a tungsten bronze-type structure and adjusts its composition by incorporating bismuth and germanium.

The sample studied combines a dielectric constant close to 185 with low losses and a reduced variation of capacitance with temperature. The authors also fabricate capacitor prototypes to examine the application of the material. The algorithm helps guide the search, but the conclusion also rests on how the samples are synthesized and characterized, an important distinction when comparing predicted materials with real components.

J. Qi et al., «Temperature-stable high-κ oxides for miniaturized capacitors», Nature Electronics, published electronically on October 6, 2026. Open Access. DOI 10.1038/s41928-026-01721-1.
