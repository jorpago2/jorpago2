Week of March 25, 2026

An infrared modulator, a fault-tolerant memory and a ferroelectric solar cell

In the mid-infrared, a small phase shift can write data onto the light emerging from an interferometer. Elsewhere a memory learns to work around its own faulty cells, and a solar cell lets us trace a change at a ferroelectric junction all the way to the voltage measured under illumination.

Photonics

Controlling mid-infrared light with lithium niobate on sapphire

[1] P. Didier et al., “Thin film lithium niobate on sapphire for integrated mid-infrared modulator,” Nature Communications, vol. 17, no. 1, Art. no. 3050, Mar. 31, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-026-69880-5.

Participating institutions
ETH Zürich (Switzerland)

Let a beam travel through two lithium niobate arms and bring the light back together. A small voltage in an arm changes the phase with which it arrives and, through interference, the intensity that leaves. The least common detail here is the colour. On its sapphire platform, the team manages to do so between 3.95 and 4.5 µm, in the middle infrared.

The prototype exceeds 20 GHz of electrical bandwidth and transmits data at 10 Gbit/s in a test. For spectroscopy, it is also useful that the same device can generate a frequency comb in that part of the spectrum.

In the comb test, the lines generated occupy about 70 GHz from one end to the other. If you go back up to 10 Gbit/s, you'll see that both numbers come out of the same piece, although one counts bits transmitted per second and the other measures how much the spectrum spreads. To send a message, the first thing is important. If what you want is to illuminate a sample with various frequencies, you will immediately start looking at the second.

Related reading. Another use of lithium niobate modulators, this time with individual photons (Light: Science & Applications).

Electronics

A memory that learns where its own defects are

[2] C. He et al., “A hardware-adaptive learning algorithm for superlinear-capacity associative memory on memristor crossbars,” Nature Communications, vol. 17, no. 1, Art. no. 3096, Mar. 31, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-026-69958-0.

Participating institutions
The University of Hong Kong (Hong Kong, China)

Imagine that you train a memory to complete a drawing with missing strokes and then discover that several cells have been blocked. You could insist on writing the correct weight over and over again, but whatever current those cells allow will continue to flow through its column. The team tries to give that information to the algorithm while it can still adjust the neighbouring weights.

With half the elements affected by stuck-at faults, the method stores roughly three times as many patterns as the study’s baseline. The comparison depends on the patterns and distribution of faults, so the test design matters alongside the headline figure.

Thus, two matrices manufactured with the same design may require different training even if they are going to recognize the same drawings. In one it will be necessary to compensate for an element that always conducts and in another one that almost does not allow current to pass. It seems to me to be a good example of why it is convenient to teach the algorithm the part it is going to use. The fault list, which at first seemed like just bad news, also tells you where you can fix the error.

Related reading. On a Model of Associative Memory with Huge Storage Capacity. Journal of Statistical Physics (2017).

Materials

A ferroelectric junction helps separate charges in a solar cell

[3] N. Wu et al., “High Open-Circuit Voltage–Fill factor product in perovskite solar cells enabled by ferroelectric heterojunction modulation,” Nature Communications, vol. 17, no. 1, Art. no. 2897, Mar. 27, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-026-69391-3.

Participating institutions
Zhejiang Normal University (China)

Having a solar cell absorb a photon is just the beginning. To take advantage of it, you have to ensure that the charges it creates reach the contacts before recombining. The team introduces a ferroelectric junction whose internal field helps separate them and looks at where the difference is noticeable in the current and voltage curve.

The open-circuit voltage rises from 1.16 to 1.21 V and the fill factor from 83.6% to 86.8%. The latter tells us what fraction of the rectangle defined by the short-circuit current and open-circuit voltage is available at the maximum power point. The best cell has a certified efficiency of 26.07%.

There is another test that I find to be as informative as measuring the best cell. The team leaves the devices working at the maximum power point and, after 500 hours, they retain more than 85% of the initial efficiency. Certification gives a good snapshot of what a cell performed at its best. Keeping it delivering power for hours answers the question that comes later, how much of that improvement remains when the part has been working for a while.

Related reading. Photo-ferroelectric perovskite interfaces for boosting VOC in efficient perovskite solar cells. Nature Communications (2024).
