Guide, remember and change with light

Week of May 13-19, 2026

A MoS₂ waveguide that also processes light

In a photonic circuit, waveguides are usually responsible for transporting light between components that perform different functions. Zhang and colleagues explore a more ambitious possibility, using the waveguide material itself to introduce a nonlinear response that can be used in an optical network.

The team manufactures molybdenum disulfide waveguides without resorting to subsequent transfer and studies their saturable absorption in telecommunications bands. This property makes the response of the material depend on the intensity of the light, so that a signal can undergo a transformation that would be difficult to achieve in a completely linear waveguide. The authors relate this response to states associated with edges and defects, and study response times of the order of ten picoseconds.

The collaboration includes KAUST, in Saudi Arabia, and Zhejiang University, in China. In addition to characterising the waveguides, the authors evaluate using representative networks how the measured response could be used as an activation function to classify images. It remains to be demonstrated how many of these waveguides would be integrated with the rest of the elements of a photonic network and what the energy cost of the complete system would be.

X. Zhang et al., Nature Communications, first epublished May 15, 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-73107-y. Open access. Q1 journal in multidisciplinary sciences according to JCR 2025. Crossref publication date. The final editorial version is later.

A photosynapse that responds to color

An artificial synapse is most interesting when its conductance can increase and decrease in a controlled manner, since both changes are necessary to adjust the weights of a network. In the work of Kim and colleagues, this possibility is addressed through a heterostructure that combines AgBiS₂ with an organic semiconductor called Y6 and responds differently depending on the wavelength used.

The device takes advantage of a persistent photoconductivity associated with the disorder of AgBiS₂. According to the team's measurements, near-infrared excitation favors an increase in conductance, while blue light accelerates its decrease. The idea is suggestive because the colour of the illumination becomes a memory control variable, along with the electrical signals used in some of the tests.

Researchers at Sungkyunkwan University in South Korea also use the measured responses in learning simulations, where they study how to balance the strengthening and weakening of weights. It is important to maintain this distinction, since experiments characterize the device and its conductance states, while the behavior of a broader network is analyzed through simulation.

H. W. Kim et al., Nature Communications, May 18, 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-73235-5. Open access. Q1 journal in multidisciplinary sciences according to JCR 2025. Publisher publication date.

The electric field brings order to a disordered material

Amorphous chalcogenides are used in memory and selection devices, but understanding what happens at the atomic scale when abrupt conduction appears remains difficult. The absence of long-range crystalline order especially complicates the task, since an electrical measurement can show when the material switches without itself revealing how it reorganizes.

Song and colleagues study amorphous layers of germanium selenide using localized electron diffraction, electrical measurements and molecular dynamics simulations with applied field. Their results suggest that the field favors the local alignment of dipoles and the appearance of bond chains, an organization that the team relates to the formation of conducting paths and threshold switching.

Among other centers, East China Normal University, Beihang University, in China, and imec, in Belgium, participate. The interpretation combines observations of areas that retain order signals after switching with simulations of the process under the field. Therefore, rather than a direct movie of each atom moving during operation, the result offers a consistent mechanism that can guide new experiments on disordered materials.

W.-X. Song et al., Nature Communications, May 13, 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-72970-z. Open access. Q1 journal in multidisciplinary sciences according to JCR 2025. Publisher publication date.
