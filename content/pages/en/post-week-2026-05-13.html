Week of May 13, 2026

A wireless link, a magnetic junction and photons from an edge

The first paper follows a signal from a chip to a wireless link. The others ask what can be gained by bringing the interaction into one small region, where a magnetic junction builds up a response to pulses and a crystal edge can feed a single-photon emitter.

Photonics

From photonic chip to wireless link

[1] Y. Guo et al., “Minimalist terahertz wireless transceiver in integrated photonics,” Nat Commun, vol. 17, no. 1, Art. no. 4429, May 19, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-026-71081-z. Open access.

To send large amounts of information through the air, it is tempting to use increasingly higher frequencies, where very wide channels are available. However, generating and receiving signals in the subterahertz region often requires complex equipment, especially if the system has to correct for frequency and phase fluctuations from its sources. Photonics can help produce those signals, although the entire setup must also be simple enough for the solution to make sense outside the lab.

Yijun Guo and his collaborators, from Peking University and ShanghaiTech University, in China, propose an architecture that takes advantage of the relationship between an optical carrier and the signal it carries. Through residual carrier modulation and injection locking, they manage to recover frequency and phase information without resorting to the digital stages that would normally be dedicated to that task. The receiver uses a single photodetector, while the assembly incorporates a thin film lithium niobate modulator and a photodiode capable of generating the radio frequency signal.

In the experimental test they transmit up to 144 gigabits per second with a 200 GHz carrier, using commercial distributed feedback lasers. The result is interesting because it reduces some of the demands placed on optical sources and carrier recovery, although it does not eliminate all the digital processing necessary to demodulate the signal. In addition, the antennas were about 20 centimeters apart and the assembly included amplifiers and other external equipment, so we are not yet looking at a complete transceiver integrated on a single chip.

The question that the work leaves open is how to preserve this simplicity when the distance and number of users increase, or when the components must operate in less controlled conditions. Still, it shows how a decision about signal architecture can be as important as the speed of each individual device.

Electronics

A magnetic junction that builds up a response to pulses

[2] Z. Chen et al., “Nanoscale exchange-bias magnetic tunnel junctions enabled memristive synapse and leaky-integrate-fire neuron for neuromorphic computing,” Nature Communications, vol. 17, no. 1, Art. no. 4362, May 14, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-026-70802-8. Open access.

Participating institutions
Beihang University (China)

A magnetic tunnel junction is often read as a device with two possible resistance states. The team changes its coupling to antiferromagnetic material and obtains intermediate states that can be programmed with repeated pulses.

Time-resolved measurements show gradual changes with 0.4 ns pulses. Depending on how it is used, the behaviour can represent a connection weight or accumulate input until the circuit emits a pulse of its own.

How memristor dynamics can be used for computation (Nature Reviews Materials).

Materials

Perovskite edges offer a route to single-photon emission

[3] G. Na, J. Y. Park, J.-P. So, P. Guo, L. Dou, and H.-G. Park, “Single-photon emission from two-dimensional perovskites channeled through low-energy edge states,” Nature Communications, vol. 17, no. 1, Art. no. 4317, May 13, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-026-71000-2. Open access.

Participating institutions
Seoul National University (South Korea)

Illuminating a layered crystal with photons below its bandgap excites fewer regions in the interior. Lower-energy states at the edge can collect charge and channel it towards a localised emitter.

The team uses photon statistics to confirm single-photon emission from some points along the edge. It also obtains emitters by creating edges through strain, suggesting a way to choose where the source sits.
