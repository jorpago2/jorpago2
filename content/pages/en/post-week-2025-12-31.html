Week of December 31, 2025

Light learns to travel, remember and change direction

31 December 2025 to 6 January 2026

Once light leaves a chip, what stays behind? This week a sensor holds on to the trace of a passing object, while a modulator relies on the way its crystal grew. The third paper sent me back to the small print under an impressive infrared result to find out which wavelength the researchers had actually measured.

Photonics

A modulator with the material in its favor

[1] C. Deng et al., “Self-buffered epitaxy of barium titanate on oxide insulators enables high-performance electro-optic modulators,” Light: Science & Applications, vol. 15, no. 1, Art. no. 21, Jan. 2, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41377-025-02081-9.

Barium titanate has an appealing property if you are designing a modulator. Apply a voltage and you change the refractive index seen by the light. Now, when you try to grow a film on a chip, it is the substrate that places the first atoms. If they are poorly accommodated, you can lose a good part of the advantage that led you to choose that material.

Chenguang Deng and colleagues at Tsinghua University in China choose an insulating oxide, LSAT. At the contact, an area is formed that accommodates the film and mechanical stress stabilises regions with different polar structures. There they measure an electro-optic coefficient greater than 358 pm/V and a Curie temperature of about 200 °C.

What do you gain by moving to the device? In one arm of an interferometer, the film allows the output intensity to be changed with a half-wave voltage–length product of 0.7 V·cm at 1550 nm. Before comparing bandwidths, check how the team defines the 28 GHz limit. They allow a 6 dB drop.

Related reading. A Barium Titanate‐on‐Oxide Insulator Optoelectronics Platform. Advanced Materials (2021).

Electronics

A photosynapse that looks in several bands

[2] G.-H. Dun et al., “Ultra-wide spectrum photosynapse array with 64k-scale for neuromorphic fusion imaging,” Nature Communications, vol. 16, no. 1, Art. no. 10699, Jan. 6, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-025-66810-9.

Think about the trail that an object leaves when crossing a camera with long exposure. At a glance you know where it has passed, even if it is no longer there. Guan-Hua Dun and colleagues at Tsinghua University and Xi'an Jiaotong University in China are looking for something similar in the stream of a pixel. The carbon nanotubes collect light in several bands and the molybdenum oxide retains charge, so it takes a while for the signal to return to its starting value.

To go from one pixel to 256 × 256, you must ensure that the film covers different materials equally. A treatment with polydopamine helps them deposit it. They test four wavelengths, from 365 nm to 10.6 µm, and combine visible and infrared imaging.

The beauty of the motion test is that you can follow the distribution of work. The sensor keeps a more intense electrical trail where the object has just passed. This trail is then digitized and a neural network names the trajectory, among the few that were tested.

Related reading. How memristor dynamics can be used for computation (Nature Reviews Materials).

Materials

A crystal that separates light also into the infrared

[3] Q. Ren et al., “[Hg3Se2]2- cluster drives giant optical anisotropy and broad infrared transparency,” Nature Communications, vol. 17, no. 1, Art. no. 40, Jan. 6, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-025-66148-2.

If someone offered you a crystal to separate two polarisations in the infrared, you would ask for two very specific pieces of information. Does the light you want to use pass through? And at that same wavelength, what difference in index do the two polarisations see? Qixian Ren and colleagues at the Xinjiang Institute of Physics and Chemistry of Chinese Academy of Sciences, China, have measured both in Hg₁₈Ga₈Se₈Cl₃₂. Their calculations point to linear groupings of mercury and selenium as the origin of the strong birefringence.

The crystal allows light to pass through approximately between 0.4 and 25 µm. Within this window, the measured index difference is worth 0.517 at 1.064 µm, 0.468 at 1.54 µm and still 0.453 at 3.5 µm. The figure that attracts the most attention, 0.871, comes from another test, with visible light of 546 nm and the sample cooled to 90 K.

If I were choosing an optical component, I would start with the value measured closest to my working wavelength. Next would come two workshop questions, what size uniform crystal can be grown and how to machine a material that contains mercury.

Related reading. Chain‐like [S x ] ( x =2–6) Units Realizing Giant Birefringence with Transparency in the Near‐Infrared for Optoelectronic Materials. Angewandte Chemie (2023).
