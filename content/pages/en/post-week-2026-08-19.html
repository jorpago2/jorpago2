Light that calculates and materials that change the rules

Week of August 19-25, 2026

A shortcut connection inside a photonic chip

In a deep neural network, not all information has to go through each operation before reaching the next stage. Residual connections allow part of the signal to take a more direct path and then be combined with the processed result. Quan et al, from Huazhong University of Science and Technology, in China, have taken that idea to an integrated photonic circuit that performs convolutions and preserves its weights without continuous power.

The device divides the information between different wavelengths. Four channels perform the convolution and a fifth carries the shortcut signal, so the contributions can be added to the output. To adjust the weights, the researchers turn to a small region of phase-change material, whose transmission changes when its state is changed by optical pulses. The team tests the operation with images and demonstrates fast residual convolution, as well as keeping two programming states separate after 300 cycles.

The demonstration also allows you to see how much work goes into the chip itself. Images are prepared and encoded with external electronics and modulators, while detection and other layers of the network are performed electronically. Therefore, although photonic operation is the center of the experiment, the result is not yet equivalent to a complete neural network functioning autonomously with light.

Z. Quan et al., Light: Science & Applications, August 19, 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41377-026-02325-2. Open access. Q1 journal in optics according to JCR 2025. Date of first publication in Crossmark.

A diode whose response is gate-tuned

In some devices, increasing the voltage can cause the current to decrease rather than increase. This negative differential resistance is interesting for designing circuits with functions that are not achieved in the same way with a conventional switch, although controlling the area in which it appears is part of the problem. Mitra et al study it in a junction between two layered semiconductors, MoTe₂ and SnS₂, whose response is modified by two gate electrodes.

The junction can rectify the current in one direction or the other depending on the applied voltages and, under other conditions, present the region of negative differential resistance. To interpret the measurements, the authors combine the experiments with electronic calculations and relate this latter behavior mainly to tunneling transport between states of the valence bands. The collaboration includes Chalmers University of Technology, in Sweden, and the universities Paris Cité and Paris-Saclay, in France.

The signal is maintained up to 150 K under the reported conditions, a figure that makes it clear that this is still a low-temperature demonstration. Thus, before thinking about practical applications, it would be necessary to check whether a comparable response can be reproducibly obtained at room temperature and in integrated devices.

R. Mitra et al., Scientific Reports, first electronically published August 24, 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-68365-1. Open access. Q1 journal in multidisciplinary sciences according to JCR 2025. The publisher later published a correction of affiliations and corresponding authors, which does not modify the original date.

Perovskites that convert infrared to color

When a material absorbs several photons together, it can emit light of higher energy than each of the incident photons. This conversion is attractive for working at infrared wavelengths, although high-order responses are often weak. Xu et al have studied three layered hybrid perovskites in which the confinement of the excitons favors a particularly strong multiphoton response.

The crystals combine inorganic and organic layers, and each composition produces a different emission in red, green or blue. By exciting materials in the infrared, researchers identify absorption processes that reach up to six photons and observe that the emitted intensity depends on the orientation of the polarization. To explain this behavior, they propose a mechanism based on the dynamics of excitonic dipoles and contrast it with simulations.

The study brings together the universities of Fuzhou, Nanjing and Nanjing University of Science and Technology, in China. Although the authors propose possible uses in imaging and photonics, the experiments presented characterize materials under laser excitation, so these applications still need specific validation. Furthermore, all three compositions contain lead, an aspect that should be taken into account when evaluating biological uses.

Y. Xu et al., Light: Science & Applications, August 25, 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41377-026-02286-6. Open access. Q1 journal in optics according to JCR 2025.
