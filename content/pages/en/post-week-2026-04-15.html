Week of April 15, 2026

Light pulses, spins and large crystals

Photonics

A cell for light-controlled neurons and synapses

C. Lu et al., “Accelerating spiking neural networks with photonic reconfigurable devices”, Nature Communications, vol. 17, art. 5367, 2026. Epub April 17. Open-access article.

Among the participants is Fudan University, in China.

In a spiking neural network, the elements that accumulate signals and those that pass signals between them usually occupy different places. Lu and colleagues investigate what happens when a single cell can perform both functions, combining a transient electrical response with a second response programmed by light.

The device incorporates hafnium zirconium oxide along with titanium dioxide. According to the authors, the electrical response allows us to emulate the behavior of a neuron that integrates pulses until a threshold is reached, while illumination provides another way to modify the state associated with a synapse. In addition, they tested optical addressing in a 16 by 16 cell submatrix, with the intention of changing the function of specific elements without altering their neighbors.

The latency and energy improvements presented in the article for vision networks come from an architectural evaluation under comparison assumptions defined by the authors, and should not be read as measured consumption of a complete network already manufactured. The experimental contribution is in the cell and its addressing, while the simulations allow us to explore what might be gained by transferring that behaviour to a larger system.

Electronics

An Ising machine with memory and a fluctuating magnet

M. A. Iftakher et al., “Intrinsic annealing in a hybrid memristor-magnetic tunnel junction Ising machine”, Nature Communications, vol. 17, art. 5246, 2026. Epub April 16. Open-access article.

The team includes researchers from Université Paris-Saclay and Université Grenoble Alpes, in France.

Many optimisation problems can be expressed as a search for the configuration that minimises a function. Ising machines transfer that idea to a physical system with states equivalent to spins and connections between them. To prevent the search from becoming trapped too soon in a local solution, fluctuations can be introduced at the beginning and reduced later, much like a gradual cooling process.

Iftakher and his collaborators combine a hafnium oxide memristor array, which stores the couplings, with a stochastic magnetic tunnel junction, which provides the probabilistic response. Increasing the reading voltage also changes the bias of the junction, so that the same signal used to read the array also helps reduce randomness gradually.

The prototype found the optimum in a 24-vertex weighted MAX-CUT problem and in a ten-vertex coloring problem. This demonstration used an externally implemented feedback loop, so the prospect of integrating larger systems is not yet equivalent to having built a complete, self-contained solver on a single chip. The result shows, however, how two memory technologies can provide complementary pieces of the same calculation.

Materials

Growing perovskite crystals with the help of a polymer

J. Sun et al., “Polymer-templated mesoscale assembly directs synthesis of centimetre-scale perovskite single crystals”, Nature Communications, vol. 17, art. 5435, 2026. Epub April 20. Open-access article.

Among the participating institutions are Zhejiang University and Westlake University, in China.

A large single crystal allows the properties of a semiconductor to be studied without the boundaries between small grains necessarily dominating the measurement. In metal halide perovskites, however, controlling how the components of the solution group together before the crystal begins to grow is difficult, because many nuclei can appear at once and produce disordered growth.

Sun and his collaborators use a polymer to influence that early stage. Interactions with precursors are reversible and favor the formation of intermediate groupings, with the aim of reducing random nucleation and facilitating subsequent organisation. With this strategy they obtained centimeter-sized single crystals in a matter of hours and tested various perovskite compositions.

The work is attractive for studying charge transport, defects and optical response with good quality samples, although a large crystal alone does not guarantee the performance or stability of a solar cell made with a thin film. Its main contribution lies in controlling nucleation, a stage of synthesis less visible than the final crystal but important in determining what can be achieved afterwards.
