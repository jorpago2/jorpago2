Week of September 16, 2026

Computing with light, retaining polarisation and turning a mask into an emitter

How much energy does an optical processor need to hold its settings while waiting for new data? A ferroelectric modulator retains its written state, and a second memory exploits the shape of its domains. In the third paper one layer also does two jobs, since the material patterned by lithography goes on to emit light.

Photonics

Keeping a photonic processor’s settings in its modulators

[1] W. Zhou et al., “Ultrafast reconfigurable topological-modulators-based photonic processing accelerator,” Nature Communications, vol. 17, no. 1, Art. no. 9920, Sep. 17, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-026-75993-8.

Participating institutions
Shanghai Jiao Tong University (China)

In the lead zirconate titanate modulators, ferroelectric polarization retains the optical setting, so the chip can hold a weight while waiting for the next data. The design concentrates light in the active region so that state can control the channels taking part in a computation.

A 16-channel prototype is tested on tasks including digit classification. Keeping the configuration inside the modulators reduces the power needed to hold it. This applies to static operation, while loading data and reading results still have a cost.

Electronics

A memory that preserves polarization loops

[2] F.-H. Gong et al., “Creation of ferroelectric flux-closure transistor array for non-volatile field-effect memory,” Nature Communications, vol. 17, no. 1, Art. no. 11009, Sep. 21, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-026-77913-2.

Participating institutions
Hong Kong Polytechnic University (Hong Kong, China), Zhejiang University (China), Hunan University of Science and Technology (China), Lanzhou University of Technology (China)

Within a ferroelectric film the polarization can turn from one domain to the next until it forms a closed loop, reducing the electric field extending outside the film. Gong and his collaborators study how to harness these structures in a field-effect memory, a problem that requires preserving them when the film is incorporated into a device.

To do this, they prepare ferroelectric films independent of the growth substrate and integrate them into transistor matrices. According to the authors, the closure structures preserve a response with a low coercive field and allow reversible write and erase operations.

The endurance tests on the ferroelectric structures reach ten billion cycles. Once the film is part of a transistor, the readout poses a different question. The polarization must leave two channel currents far enough apart to identify the written state, and the measured on/off ratio reaches one hundred million.\n\nThe work brings together researchers from institutions of the Chinese Academy of Sciences and other universities in China.

Materials

A material that serves as a mask and also emits light

[3] S.-W. Lo et al., “Electroluminescent photoresists extending lithographic scaling to OLEDs,” Nature, vol. 658, no. 8134, pp. 78–85, Sep. 16, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41586-026-11042-0.

The resist that defines a pixel during lithography is usually stripped away before the device begins to operate. Lo and his collaborators propose a material that retains a second function after the pattern, since it can also emit light when incorporated into an organic diode.

The polymers have a core-shell architecture that protects the emitter while the outer regions participate in the cross-linking reactions necessary to form the pattern. The team demonstrated sequential multilayer processing, fluorescent structures with dimensions down to 110 nanometers, and multicolor OLEDs with external quantum efficiencies greater than 13% in the characterized devices.

The 110 nm figure is the finest detail in a fluorescent pattern, whereas the efficiency above 13% was measured in electrically driven OLEDs. Connecting the two figures for a display would require measuring efficiency as the emissive pixel itself is made smaller.\n\nAmong the participating institutions is ETH Zürich, in Switzerland.
