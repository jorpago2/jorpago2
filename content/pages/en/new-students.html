Starting research

Could research be right for you?

Scientific research does not require a single personality type or a fixed checklist. Curiosity, persistence and confidence all develop through practice.

A more useful question is whether you enjoy asking why, working through uncertainty and improving ideas that are not yet complete. Claude Shannon’s 1952 lecture “Creative Thinking” offers a helpful way to think about these habits.

Four useful research habits

These are habits to develop, not admission criteria you must already meet.

  1. Curiosity and drive

    A genuine desire to understand how things work matters more than already knowing every answer.

  2. Constructive dissatisfaction

    Research often begins by noticing that an explanation, measurement or design could be improved.

  3. Reframing

    Looking at a problem from different viewpoints can reveal hidden assumptions and possible solutions.

  4. Decomposition

    Large problems become tractable when they are divided into clear, testable steps.
