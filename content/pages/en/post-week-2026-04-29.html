Week of April 29, 2026

Reflecting polaritons, a graphene gate and dipoles that turn

An edge reflects a wave travelling across a flake, a graphene gate controls a current through molecules, and electrodes read out a vertical polarization. In each experiment, the direction of the measurement affects what can be learned about the material.

Photonics

Following a polariton until it reflects off an edge

[1] A. Ghosh et al., “Spatiotemporal visualization of long-range anisotropic plasmon polaritons in hyperbolic MoOCl2,” Nature Communications, vol. 17, no. 1, Art. no. 3884, Apr. 29, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-026-70565-2.

Participating institutions
The University of Chicago (United States)

A still image gives little sense of how a wave of coupled light and electronic charge reaches the edge of a MoOCl₂ flake. With time-resolved photoemission microscopy, the team follows them as they propagate and reflect from the edges.

Some modes travel more than 10 µm, around three times the distance of more tightly confined modes reported in the same material. That comparison shows how much the choice of mode changes the distance a signal can travel within a single flake.

Reflection gives access to more than the distance travelled. Images recorded at different delays distinguish a front approaching the edge from one returning. A static intensity map could contain both at once and make them look like a single wave, so resolving time changes what can be inferred from the same piece of crystal.

Related reading. Manipulating Hyperbolic Plasmon Polaritons at Near-Infrared in an Anisotropic van der Waals Crystal. Nano Letters (2025).

Electronics

A molecular monolayer under a graphene gate

[2] Y. Xie et al., “High-performance microelectronic-integratable molecular transistors,” Nature Communications, vol. 17, no. 1, Art. no. 5871, Apr. 29, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-026-72473-x.

A transistor made from a molecular monolayer between two electrodes needs a gate that can control the monolayer’s electronic levels reproducibly from one junction to the next.

Yu Xie and his collaborators, with the participation of the Tsinghua University in China, have studied vertical transistors in which a self-assembled monolayer is placed between a gold electrode and a graphene electrode. An ionic liquid on the graphene acts as a gate and allows the position of the molecular levels to be adjusted with respect to the energy of the electrons in the graphene. The authors test molecules of two types and find ratios between the on and off currents greater than 10⁴, even at temperatures up to 350 K.

The team also fabricates about 8,000 structures on a two-inch wafer and obtains a functional transistor yield of 73% with the criteria it defines for evaluation. This figure must be distinguished from the manufacturing efficiency of the junctions, which is higher because not all the junctions that are formed later function as a transistor with the required performance.

The liquid gate lets the team study transport through the monolayer and obtain an on/off current ratio above ten thousand. Turning this into a circuit poses a wiring problem. Addressing one transistor among many would mean setting its gate independently of its neighbours.

Related reading. Realization of Molecular‐Based Transistors. Advanced Materials (2018).

Materials

Changing the direction of polarization

[3] S. Zhou et al., “Templated perpendicular ferroelectricity in textured Aurivillius oxide-based thin films,” Nature Communications, vol. 17, no. 1, Art. no. 3890, Apr. 29, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-026-70676-w.

Electrodes placed above and below a ferroelectric film are well positioned to read a polarization pointing from one face to the other. Bismuth tungstate belongs to a family of layered oxides whose polarization tends to lie in the plane of the layers, a feature that can limit certain device geometries.

The study by Zhou and colleagues, involving Zhejiang University and the Institute of Physics of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, in China, proposes using the material’s own structure as a template to stabilize a metastable phase of tungsten oxide. The result is a film with out-of-plane polarization, whose structure is analysed using atomic-resolution microscopy and calculations.

The authors also fabricated test ferroelectric transistors and memristors, observing switching ratios above one million and thermal stability up to 350 °C in the tests described.

Related reading. Observation of Hidden Polar Phases and Flux Closure Domain Topology in Bi2WO6 Thin Films. Nano Letters (2023).
