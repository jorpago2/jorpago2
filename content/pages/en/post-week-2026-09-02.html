Week of September 2, 2026

A nonlinear metasurface, high-temperature vision and an adjustable Hall response

Nonlinearity in a metasurface provides a way to transform light. The other papers examine two electrical responses worth controlling, in a sensor that still detects light in extreme heat and a junction whose polarisation affects how current is deflected.

Photonics

A metasurface that gives access to nonlinearity

[1] P. U. Fathi et al., “Quantum-well metasurface for free-space-accessible enhanced nonlinear polarization,” Nature Nanotechnology, vol. 21, no. 11, pp. 1558–1564, Sep. 2, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41565-026-02268-0.

Converting light from one frequency to another usually requires materials with a strong nonlinear response or paths long enough to accumulate the effect. If we want to reduce the device to a thin film, one option is to design the material and the structure that illuminates it so that both work together.

Pernille Undrum Fathi and her collaborators, with participation from Harvard University in the United States, combine GaAs and AlGaAs quantum wells with a dielectric metasurface. The wells are designed to reinforce a transition between electronic states, while the upper structure modifies the optical fields and facilitates access to components of the nonlinear response that would be difficult to exploit with direct illumination.

Putting a number on the response requires specifying the wavelength. The quantum wells are designed around an electronic transition and the metasurface concentrates the field through a resonance, so changing the laser frequency also changes the strength of the interaction.

At 1.57 µm the quantum wells provide a second-order susceptibility component of 1.6 nm/V. Patterning the metasurface raises the effective response of the assembly to about 14 nm/V. Part of the increase therefore comes from how the patterned structure distributes the optical field.

Electronics

A sensor with memory that still works at extreme temperatures

[2] Y. Zou et al., “Atomically thin heterojunction-based optoelectronic synaptic devices operable from −190 °C to 450 °C,” Nature Communications, vol. 17, no. 1, Art. no. 9430, Sep. 3, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-026-76364-z.

Participating institutions
Tsinghua University (China)

Sensing light at high temperatures is difficult when heat alters electronic transport or speeds up charge loss. The team designs the interface of a two-dimensional junction based on MoS₂ so its light response and memory can be adjusted.

Devices are tested between −190 and 450 °C, and still detect visible light at 450 °C. The experiment establishes the operating interval under controlled conditions, a starting point for testing an array in an actual environment.

Materials

A Hall response that changes with ferroelectric polarization

[3] R. Wang et al., “Room-temperature ferroelectrically switchable quantum geometry in few-layer WTe2 for complementary in-memory computing,” Nature Communications, vol. 17, no. 1, Art. no. 9466, Sep. 4, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-026-76369-8.

Among the participants is Nanjing University, in China.

In a ferroelectric material, the polarization can retain an orientation after removing the field that changed it. Wang and his collaborators explore how this electrical memory is related to the nonlinear Hall response of a few layers of WTe₂, where the measured signal also depends on geometric properties of the electronic states.

By changing the polarization at room temperature, the team observes second- and third-order responses that switch in a correlated manner and with opposite signs. The authors study how to use these complementary signals to extract edges in different orientations, and describe a nine-element configuration for a convolution operation. They also report retention of the order of 100,000 seconds and tests of more than 10,000 cycles under work conditions.

In the texture recognition evaluation, the accuracy of 98% corresponds to an ideal simulation of the network, while when incorporating measured features and noise from the devices the figure drops to 94%. The distinction helps place the scope of the result, which combines an interesting physical answer with an analysis of how it could be used in in-memory computing, without that evaluation being equivalent to a complete processor already deployed.
