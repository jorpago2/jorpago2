Research career

Five principles for building a research career

This is the personal framework I use to choose questions, develop a coherent profile and match scientific ambitions to the resources available in Spain and Europe.

It is not a universal formula. Career paths, evaluation criteria and funding opportunities vary by discipline, institution and career stage.

  1. Choose meaningful and feasible problems

    I look for questions at the intersection of scientific relevance, realistic execution and sustained personal motivation. Ambition matters, but so does having a credible route to evidence.

    Question I ask: Is this problem worth solving, and can I identify a rigorous first step?

  2. Build a coherent, flexible identity

    A research identity connects previous work with a convincing next direction. Coherence helps others understand my contribution, while flexibility leaves room for new methods, collaborations and questions.

    Question I ask: What connects my projects beyond a technique, material or application?

  3. Publish for rigour and the right audience

    I prioritise reliable methods, clear evidence and venues read by the community I want to reach. Metrics can provide context, but they do not replace an assessment of the work itself.

    Question I ask: Does this paper make a trustworthy and recognisable contribution?

  4. Develop reciprocal collaborations

    Independence does not mean isolation. Constructive collaborations, honest feedback and mentoring broaden my perspective and help me avoid preventable mistakes.

    Question I ask: What can each person contribute and learn through this collaboration?

  5. Match resources to the next milestone

    Funding paths are rarely linear. I select calls according to the scientific milestone, eligibility rules and resources required, while clarifying facilities, support and teaching commitments before accepting a position.

    Question I ask: What is the smallest credible resource package that moves the research forward?
